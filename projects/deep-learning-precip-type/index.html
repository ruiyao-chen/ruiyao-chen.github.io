Deep learning for precipitation type from microwave sounders

CNN, MLP and random-forest models that classify hydrometeor type (liquid, mixed, ice) and estimate rain rate from 89–190 GHz satellite observations, trained on GPM radar labels and grounded in radiative-transfer physics.

84.7% classification accuracy on held-out data (Remote Sensing, 2023)

2019 – 2023 · Vanderbilt
  • PyTorch
  • CNN with attention
  • random forest
  • classification
  • regression
  • passive microwave
Paper (Remote Sensing, 2023)

Microwave humidity sounders such as FY-3C MWHS-2 see through clouds to precipitation ice and liquid, but turning their brightness temperatures into precipitation type and rate is a non-linear problem with weak physical constraints. I built a sequence of models for it:

  • Physics first. A radiative-transfer sensitivity study of 89–190 GHz channels to ice-particle scattering (JAMC, 2020) defined which channels and features carry the signal.
  • Rain rate. Rainfall algorithms for oceanic MWHS-2 observations (Advances in Atmospheric Sciences, 2021), with machine-learning models that outperformed non-linear regression baselines.
  • Precipitation type. A convolutional neural network with an attention mechanism, trained on hydrometeor labels from the GPM Dual-frequency Precipitation Radar (Remote Sensing, 2023). It reaches 84.7% accuracy on test data, and its global annual cycles of precipitation occurrence agree with the radar's.

This work became the foundation for the operational TROPICS retrieval, which deliberately trades the neural network for an instance-based method that cannot extrapolate beyond its training data.
