A consistent 20-year satellite record, by finding hidden sensor errors

Root-caused two hidden on-orbit anomalies in NASA's 17-year TRMM Microwave Imager record and designed a three-way cross-calibration that ties TRMM and GPM into one consistent multi-decadal data record.

Corrections adopted in NASA's TMI legacy reprocessing; TMI–WindSat stable to 0.1 K over 5+ years

2012 – 2018 · Ph.D., University of Central Florida
  • sensor calibration
  • uncertainty modeling
  • radiative transfer
  • data-record consistency
  • MATLAB

Climate-scale datasets are stitched together from many satellites. If the sensors disagree, the "trend" in the record is partly an artifact. My Ph.D. work made NASA's precipitation microwave record consistent across two decades.

  • Found two hidden anomalies in the TRMM Microwave Imager: solar heating of the hot-load calibration target and an emissive main reflector. I built physics-based correction models fit to flight data, which were adopted in the TMI legacy (1B11 V8) reprocessing (IEEE JSTARS, 2018).
  • Three-way cross-calibration of GMI, TMI and WindSat with the NASA GPM inter-calibration working group, using radiative-transfer simulations as the common reference. TMI and WindSat stayed consistent to 0.1 K over more than five years (IEEE JSTARS, 2017; JTECH, 2016).
  • A generic uncertainty model for sensor inter-calibration, used to put an error bar on the merged TRMM–GPM record (Climate, 2020).

The work contributed to a NASA Agency Group Achievement Award (2015), and the same pattern of comparing sensors against a shared reference with an explicit uncertainty budget runs through my later projects.
